The Trip at a Glance

The Big Picture

A thoughtfully paced 11-night journey through three of Japan's most iconic destinations. You'll bookend the trip with Tokyo (modern, electric, deeply rewarding), spend three nights in Kyoto for temples, gardens, and quieter culture, then retreat to a Lake Ashi ryokan where Mt. Fuji rises across the water from your private onsen. All Tokyo and Kyoto stays are Marriott Bonvoy properties — the Hakone leg is a traditional Japanese inn that's worth the detour from the points trail.

Timing Note

Mid-May is one of the loveliest stretches in Japan — Golden Week crowds have dispersed, the weather is mild (60–75°F), and you'll catch the tail of the wisteria/azalea season. Best of all, the May Grand Sumo Tournament runs May 10–24 at Ryōgoku Kokugikan in Tokyo — built right into Day 3 of the itinerary.

HOTEL THE MITSUI KYOTO ↗
May 13 – 16 (3 nights)

Awarded Three Michelin Keys in 2025 and named to The World's 50 Best Hotels — and rightly so. Built on a 250-year-old Mitsui family site directly across from Nijō Castle, the hotel has its own natural hot spring drawn from 1,000 meters below ground. The communal "Thermal Spring" pools (swimsuits worn) are unlike anything you'll find at another hotel — a perfect end-of-day soak after temple touring. Rooms are serene, the breakfast is excellent, and Chef Asano's Kyoto-French restaurant TOKI is a destination in itself.

Try Yuyado Zen first — its lake-and-Fuji-from-your-bath experience is the emotional centerpiece of the trip. Kowakien Ten-yu is the safest Plan B (similar luxury, much more availability, but no lake). Yoshimatsu is third only if you can secure the single Fuji-view room.

Day by Day

The Itinerary

Each day is paced with one or two anchor activities, a leisurely lunch window, and an evening that's never rushed. Most days end before 7 pm so there's room for a relaxed dinner and an early-ish night. Mornings are deliberately light to start.

01 Saturday, May 9 — Arrival

Tokyo · Easy landing

Afternoon
Land in Tokyo
Most US flights arrive at Haneda (HND) — closer and easier than Narita. The Airport Limousine Bus goes directly to Mesm Tokyo's neighborhood (Takeshiba/Hamamatsucho) in about 30 minutes; about ¥1,000 per person. A taxi runs ¥6,500–8,000 if luggage is heavy.
Late Afternoon
Settle in at Mesm Tokyo
Quick reset in the room. If energy is up, walk five minutes next door to Hamarikyu Gardens — a 17th-century shogunal garden surrounded by skyscrapers, with a teahouse on a pond. ¥300 entry, open until 5 pm.
Evening
Dinner at the hotel or nearby
Don't fight the jet lag. Have a low-key dinner at Chef's Theatre in the hotel (excellent open-kitchen tasting menu) or order room service and unwind. Tomorrow is the real start.
02 Sunday, May 10 — Old Tokyo & teamLab

Asakusa · Sensō-ji · teamLab Planets

Morning
Sensō-ji Temple & Nakamise Street Iconic
Tokyo's oldest Buddhist temple (founded 645 AD). Enter through the giant red Kaminarimon lantern gate, walk Nakamise Dori with its 89 stalls of crackers, fans, and sweets, and arrive at the main hall. Get there by 9 am to beat the tour groups. Allow about 90 minutes.
Lunch
Asakusa Imahan — sukiyaki No fish
A 130-year-old institution serving classic sukiyaki — premium beef cooked tableside in cast iron with sweet-soy broth, dipped in raw egg. Lighter lunch sets available. Walking distance from Sensō-ji, no reservation needed but go right at noon to beat the wave.

Alt: Sometaro for okonomiyaki — Japanese cabbage-and-pork pancakes you cook yourself on a tabletop griddle. Casual and fun.
Afternoon
teamLab Planets Tokyo Reservation From Wanderlog
Taxi 20 min from Asakusa to Toyosu — the immersive digital art experience that defines the trip for many visitors. Barefoot wading through warm water, mirrored infinity rooms full of LED orchids, a koi pond projected onto your feet. About 90 minutes inside. Book the 3:00 or 3:30 pm slot online — sells out a week ahead.
Evening
Dinner: Ginza Kagari (soba) Reservation From Wanderlog
A perfect first proper Tokyo dinner — refined chicken-broth soba in a tiny Ginza basement, one of Grant's favorites from his trip. Reserve via Pocket Concierge or TableCheck two weeks ahead. 10-min walk from Mesm Tokyo.
03 Monday, May 11 — Imperial Tokyo & Sumo

Imperial Palace · Tsukiji · Grand Sumo Tournament

Morning
Imperial Palace East Gardens
The grounds of the former Edo Castle, free entry, mostly flat paths through stone walls and Japanese gardens. About 90 minutes is plenty. Beautiful azalea blooms in mid-May.
Lunch
Tsukiji Outer Market food crawl Highlight
Inner market moved to Toyosu, but the outer market is still a wonderland — tamago (sweet egg) on a stick, wagyu skewers, A5 beef croquettes, dashimaki tamago, melon-pan, fresh strawberries, oysters if they want them. Graze through 6–8 stalls. Plenty for non-sushi eaters; the cooked food stalls are the real treat anyway.
Afternoon & Evening
Grand Sumo May Tournament Book Now
Held only three times a year in Tokyo — and it's running while you're there. Doors open at 8 am but real action starts around 3:30 pm; top-division (makuuchi) bouts run 4–6 pm. Get Box B or Box C seats (4-person tatami boxes near the floor) — ¥9,500–¥11,500 per seat. Ryōgoku Kokugikan, 25 min by taxi from Mesm.

Buy tickets at: BuySumoTickets.com or sumo.or.jp (official) — release dates are typically 1 month in advance, sell out fast.
Dinner
Chanko nabe in Ryōgoku
The traditional one-pot stew sumo wrestlers eat to bulk up — surprisingly delicious and healthy. Chanko Tomoegata (run by a former wrestler) is the classic move. Walking distance from the arena.
04 Tuesday, May 12 — Lucky Cats & Modern Tokyo

Gotokuji · Shiro-Hige's · Meiji · Harajuku · Shibuya Sky

Early Morning
Gotokuji Temple — the lucky cat shrine Wanderlog favorite
The original "maneki-neko" (waving lucky cat) temple — a forested Setagaya shrine where thousands of white porcelain cats are stacked in offering. Photogenic, peaceful, totally unique. Take the Setagaya Line from Shibuya, ~25 min. Open 6 am, get there by 9 to have it nearly empty. Free entry. Allow 60 min including the walk in.
Mid-Morning
Shiro-Hige's Cream Puff Factory Studio Ghibli
The only officially licensed Studio Ghibli bakery in the world — they sell Totoro-shaped cream puffs (custard, chocolate, seasonal fillings). Hilariously cute and surprisingly delicious. Located in Setagaya, 10 min by train from Gotokuji. Pickup window only — 30 min queue typical, then walk to nearby Shoin Jinja Park to eat.
Optional
Kichijōji Petit Mura Skip if tired
A whimsical Studio Ghibli–inspired alley of fairy-tale shops in Kichijoji, on the Inokashira line — feels like wandering into a storybook. Quick stop (45 min). Combine with a brief look at Inokashira Park across the street. Skip if energy is fading and head to Shibuya for lunch.
Lunch
Katsudon-ya Zuicho — Shibuya pork cutlet bowl Wanderlog must
Tiny Shibuya counter dedicated entirely to perfecting katsudon — crispy pork cutlet over rice with egg-and-onion. No reservations, expect a 30–45 min queue, totally worth it. 5 min walk from Shibuya station. (Grant rated this as one of his Tokyo highlights.)
Early Afternoon
Meiji Jingu & Yoyogi Park From Wanderlog
Train one stop to Harajuku, then walk into the massive forested shrine — feels like leaving Tokyo entirely. Meiji Jingu Gyoen (Inner Garden) is in iris-blooming season in mid-May, easily the prettiest the garden gets all year (¥500). Then cross into Yoyogi Park next door — Sunday brings rockabilly dancers, but on a Tuesday it's just a beautiful big city park.
Mid Afternoon
Harajuku stroll & Samoyed Cafe From Wanderlog
Walk Omotesando's tree-lined avenue, cut to Takeshita Street for the teen-culture spectacle. Specific stops:
  • Samoyed Cafe moffu — pet a giant fluffy samoyed dog (reservation required online; Mom & Dad will love it)
  • Banana no Kamisama — "the banana god," premium banana smoothies on Takeshita
  • Latte Pocket — Harajuku coffee with photogenic latte art
  • Tsubaki Salon Shibuya — soufflé pancakes if there's appetite for a snack
Late Afternoon
Shibuya: MEGA Don Quijote, RURU, Shibuya Sky Sunset
Walk into Shibuya proper. Three quick stops in a 10-block radius:
  • MEGA Don Quijote Shibuya — chaotic 7-floor discount megastore (cosmetics, snacks, electronics, souvenirs). A Tokyo experience in itself.
  • RURU SHIBUYA — boutique chocolate shop / patisserie tucked nearby — beautiful gift boxes
  • Shibuya Sky — 360° open-air rooftop on top of Shibuya Scramble Square, 230m up. Book the 5:00 pm slot online to catch sunset with Mt. Fuji on a clear day — best view in central Tokyo. Cross Shibuya Scramble on the way back down.
Evening
Gyukatsu Katsugyu — beef cutlet dinner Wanderlog must
A 5-min walk from Shibuya Sky on Dogenzaka. Gyukatsu = breaded beef cutlet, served rare, sliced thin, you finish cooking each piece on a small hot stone at the table. Crispy outside, ruby inside, dipped in soy or wasabi. No raw fish, no reservations — expect a 15–30 min wait. Grant's favorite Shibuya dinner.
05 Wednesday, May 13 — Tokyo to Kyoto

Shinkansen · Pontocho stroll

Morning
Slow morning, last walk in Hamarikyu
Hotel breakfast, pack up, leave bags with the concierge. One last stroll in Hamarikyu Gardens — the rapeseed/cosmos field is in full color in May.
Midday
Shinkansen Tokyo → Kyoto · Green Car First Class
Take the Nozomi from Tokyo Station — 2 hr 14 min direct to Kyoto. Book Green Car (first class) seats, row 11 or 12 on the right side (E seat) for the best Mt. Fuji view about 45 min in. Bento boxes ("ekiben") from the Tokyo Station Daimaru basement are a tradition — pick up before boarding.

Book via: Smart-EX app or smart-ex.jp — release 1 month before, instant QR boarding.
Afternoon
Check in HOTEL THE MITSUI KYOTO
Taxi from Kyoto Station, ~15 min, ¥2,000. After check-in, head straight to the Thermal Spring spa in the basement — the perfect way to shake off the train. Swimsuit-required communal bath plus private onsen rooms by reservation.
Late Lunch
Kyoto Ramen Yukichi Honpo Wanderlog must
A 10-min walk from the hotel. Yukichi serves classic Kyoto-style chicken-broth ramen — clear, bright, paired with handmade noodles. Refined and lighter than Tokyo tonkotsu. Counter seating, no reservations. Grant's go-to first-meal in Kyoto.
Late Afternoon
Nishiki Market & Sanjo coffee crawl From Wanderlog
Walk down to Nishiki Market's 400-year-old "Kyoto's kitchen" — five blocks of stalls (yuba tofu skin, soy donuts, mochi, pickles, dashimaki tamago, grilled wagyu skewers). Plenty of non-fish bites. Then coffee at glänta Kyoto Sanjo Kawaramachi — a sleek modern coffee bar Grant loved on his trip. Walk Kiyamachi-dori & Teramachi-dori on the way back.
Evening
Pontocho Alley dinner
The lantern-lit alley along the Kamo River — Kyoto at its most cinematic. Two great picks (no raw fish):
  • Kyoto Tonkatsu Katsuda Sanjo Kawaramachi — Grant's pick, beautifully crispy Kyoto-style tonkatsu, walk-in-friendly
  • Mishima-tei Pontocho — Kyoto's legendary 150-year-old sukiyaki house, premium beef cooked tableside in cast iron, dipped in raw egg. No fish.
Many Pontocho restaurants set up yuka (riverside terraces) starting in May — request one when booking.
06 Thursday, May 14 — Arashiyama

Kinkaku-ji · bamboo grove · Sagano railway · river boat

Early Morning
Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion) Wanderlog must
Taxi from hotel ~20 min. The actual gold-leafed temple reflecting on its mirror pond — opens 9 am but get there right at opening to beat tour buses. 45 minutes inside. ¥500. From here, taxi or bus 30 min to Arashiyama.
Mid-Morning
Tenryū-ji & the Bamboo Grove From Wanderlog
Arrive Arashiyama ~10:30 am. Visit Tenryū-ji first (UNESCO Zen temple, breathtaking pond garden), then enter the famous bamboo grove from the back of its precinct — the angle most photographers don't know. The grove is genuinely stunning when the wind moves through it.
Mid-morning
Sagano Scenic Railway Romantic Train
The 25-minute open-window vintage train ride up the Hozu River gorge. Board at Torokko Saga Station, ride to Torokko Kameoka. Reserve seats in advance on Car 5 (open-air car) for the best views. ¥880 per ticket.
Lunch
Yudofu lunch — or Yamamoto Menzou for udon From Wanderlog
Two solid options depending on appetite:
  • Shōraian — specialty Buddhist yudofu (silky tofu simmered in kombu broth), dramatic dining room over the river
  • Yamamoto Menzou — Grant's pick: handmade Kyoto udon in a refined setting near central Kyoto. Skip the train back into town and have it after Hozugawa instead
Afternoon
Hozugawa River Boat Ride From Wanderlog
From Kameoka, take the 2-hour traditional wooden boat ride back down the river through the gorge — three boatmen poling and steering through small rapids. A genuine highlight. Roughly ¥4,500 per person; first boat 9 am, last 3:30 pm. (Grant did this and rated it as one of his Kyoto highlights.)
Late Afternoon
Optional: Arashiyama Monkey Park If energy
A 20-min uphill hike (steep but short) to a clearing on Mt. Arashiyama where 100+ wild Japanese macaques wander free, with one of the best panoramic views of Kyoto from the top. ¥800. Skip if knees are tired.
Evening
Kaiseki dinner at Mitsui's TOKI
A relaxed ryokan-style dinner at the hotel. Chef Asano's "Kyoto French" cuisine uses Kyoto water and seasonal produce. Otherwise an early night at the thermal spring spa — Day 7 starts before sunrise.
07 Friday, May 15 — Nara & Higashiyama

Tōdai-ji · Nara deer · Yasaka Pagoda · K36 sunset

Early Morning
Train to Nara Wanderlog must
JR Nara Line from Kyoto Station, 45 min direct via the Miyakoji Rapid. Leave by 7:30 am to be in Nara by 9. Coffee/breakfast pastry from Kyoto Station before boarding.
Morning
Tōdai-ji & Nara Park Wanderlog must
15-min walk from Nara Station to Nara Park — the famous park where 1,000+ wild but tame deer wander free. Buy "shika senbei" deer crackers (¥200) — the deer have learned to bow politely for them. Then continue to Tōdai-ji, the world's largest wooden building, housing a 15-meter-tall bronze Daibutsu (Great Buddha) that's been there since 752 AD. ¥800 entry. Allow 2 hours total. Walk back to the station via the small temples and shops along Sanjō-dori.
Lunch
Back to Kyoto · Menbaka Fire Ramen Wanderlog gem
Train back to Kyoto, ~45 min. Lunch at Menbaka Fire Ramen — the chef pours flaming hot scallion oil into your bowl tableside, the flame leaps three feet into the air. Spectacle plus genuinely good negi-shoyu ramen. They hand you a bib first. (One of Grant's Wanderlog must-tries.)
Afternoon
Higashiyama walk: Kiyomizu-dera → Sannenzaka → Hōkan-ji Pagoda From Wanderlog
Taxi to Kiyomizu-zaka. Start at Kiyomizu-dera (UNESCO temple on a wooden stage above the city, ¥400), then descend the preserved stone streets of Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka, lined with old wooden shops. Pass Hōkan-ji Temple (Yasaka Pagoda) — the iconic five-story wooden pagoda framed by old Kyoto roofs.
Dessert Crawl
Sannenzaka sweets stops Wanderlog favorites
Pace yourselves — pick 2–3 of these as you walk down Sannenzaka:
  • Maccha House — matcha soft serve and parfait, the icon of Kyoto desserts
  • Totaro — warabimochi (jelly mochi) and matcha
  • Rokujuan — translucent jelly desserts that look like falling water
  • KUMONOCHA Kiyomizu Sannenzaka — cool cloud-shaped lattes
  • (THISIS)SHIZEN — modern plant-based desserts
  • RAU Patisserie & Chocolate — chef-driven contemporary patisserie
Evening
K36 Rooftop sunset · Gion dinner Wanderlog favorite
K36 The Bar & Rooftop sits directly across from Yasaka Pagoda — Grant rated it one of the best sunset cocktail spots in Kyoto. Old roofs and the pagoda lit up below. Then continue into Gion, Kyoto's preserved geisha district — around 6 pm you may spot real geiko/maiko commuting to evening engagements. Dinner at Mishima-tei (legendary Kyoto sukiyaki, in business since 1873) or okonomiyaki at Issen Yoshoku for something casual — both fish-free.
08 Saturday, May 16 — Kyoto → Hakone

Nijō Castle · Train to ryokan country

Early Morning
Nijō Castle Across from hotel
Right across the street from the hotel — walk over before breakfast crowds. A 17th-century shogun's residence with the famous "nightingale floors" that chirp when stepped on (anti-ninja design). Beautiful gardens; about 75 minutes. ¥1,300.
Mid-Morning
glänta Ring Creation Workshop Wanderlog gem
A genuinely cool keepsake — at glänta Kyoto's atelier, you forge your own silver ring from a flat strip of metal: hammer, shape, polish, finish. About 2 hours for the experience, English-speaking instructors, walk away wearing it. Reservation required well in advance. A great take-home memory of Kyoto for Mom & Dad.

Heads up: the workshop runs long — book the 9:30 or 10 am slot to leave time for an early-afternoon train.
Lunch / Train
Shinkansen Kyoto → Odawara · 2 hr 10 min
Pick up bento boxes ("ekiben") at Kyoto Station to eat on the train. Take the Hikari to Odawara (the faster Nozomi doesn't stop there). At Odawara, transfer to a 30-min taxi or scheduled hotel pickup to Hakone Yuyado Zen on Lake Ashi.
Afternoon
Check in Hakone Yuyado Zen Mt Fuji moment
Slip into yukata robes provided by the ryokan. Long soak in the private balcony onsen looking out at Lake Ashi and Fuji. Tea and wagashi sweets in the room.
Evening
Kaiseki dinner in-room 10+ courses
Multi-course traditional dinner served by the room attendant — appetizers, simmered dishes, Hakone mountain vegetables, hot pot, grilled wagyu, rice course, dessert. A wonderful, slow ritual.

Important: contact Yuyado Zen at booking to request a no-fish kaiseki menu — they will substitute beef/chicken/vegetable courses for the fish/seafood components. Most ryokans accommodate this with 7+ days notice. After dinner, one more onsen soak under the stars.
09 Sunday, May 17 — Lake Ashi & the Crater

Hakone Shrine · pirate ship · Owakudani · Open-Air Museum

Pre-breakfast
Sunrise onsen + Hakone Shrine torii Iconic photo
Wake early. Soak. Then walk 15 minutes to Hakone Shrine — the famous floating red torii gate in Lake Ashi with Fuji behind it. Get there before 8 am or there's a 60-minute photo line. Quiet at sunrise.
Mid-morning
Hakone Sightseeing Cruise (Pirate Ship)
Yes, it's a pirate ship — kind of camp, totally fun. 35-min cruise from Motohakone-ko across Lake Ashi to Togendai. ¥1,200. The whole Hakone Round Course works in a loop and is included in the Hakone Free Pass (¥6,100, worth it).
Late Morning
Hakone Ropeway over Owakudani Volcanic valley
From Togendai, take the cable car high over the active volcanic valley — sulfur steam vents, black rock, and (on a clear day) huge Mt. Fuji views from the gondola. Get off at Owakudani Station and try the kuro-tamago (eggs hard-boiled in the sulfur springs — black shell, normal yolk, said to add 7 years to your life).
Lunch
Soba in Gora
Continue down to Gora Station. Lunch at Hatsuhana Honten (Hakone's most famous soba house, 100+ years old) — handmade buckwheat noodles in dashi.
Early Afternoon
Hakone Gora Park From Wanderlog
Restored French-style hilltop garden right next to Gora Station — fountain, rose terraces, traditional teahouse, and a glass-walled greenhouse. Pretty in May with the roses just starting. Quick 45-min stop. ¥550.
Afternoon
Hakone Open-Air Museum Highlight
70,000 sqm of outdoor sculpture park with works by Henry Moore, Rodin, Miro — plus a dedicated Picasso Pavilion with 300+ works. There's also a free hot-spring foot bath in the middle of the park. About 2 hours. ¥1,800.
Evening
Back to ryokan · second kaiseki
Different menu tonight — the chef changes the kaiseki nightly so the second dinner is a fresh experience. One more long onsen and an early bedtime.
10 Monday, May 18 — Hakone → Tokyo & Shinjuku

Romancecar · Shinjuku stops · Roppongi sunset

Morning
Final onsen, leisurely breakfast
The ryokan breakfast is itself an event — miso soup, pickles, silken tofu, dashimaki tamago, fresh egg over rice, mountain vegetables. Pack slowly. (Ask in advance for the no-fish version here too — they'll swap the grilled fish course.)
Late Morning
Romancecar Hakone-Yumoto → Shinjuku 85 min direct
The Odakyu Romancecar Limited Express — reserved seating, panoramic windows, runs hourly. Most comfortable train ride into Tokyo. About ¥2,500 + base fare. Book at e-romancecar.jp. Drops you right at Shinjuku Station, where the next stretch starts.
Afternoon (Shinjuku stop)
Shinjuku walking tour All Wanderlog favorites
Drop bags in a coin locker at Shinjuku Station (or have the EDITION send a porter). Then a 3-hour Shinjuku afternoon hitting all of Grant's neighborhood favorites:
  • Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building — Kenzo Tange's twin towers, free observation deck on the 45th floor with one of the best Tokyo Tower / Mt. Fuji panoramas in the city. Daytime hits different than the evening crowd.
  • Don Quijote Shinjuku Kabukicho — the chaotic 24-hour discount megastore. Better than the Shibuya MEGA branch for nightlife district vibes. Pick up matcha Kit-Kats, snacks, last-minute souvenirs.
  • Kumachan Onsen — a tiny themed café where a cute bear sits in a "hot tub" of your latte foam. Photogenic and silly in the best way.
  • (Optional) Shinjuku Gyoen — Tokyo's most beautiful imperial garden if there's energy left, late azalea blooms in May. ¥500.
Late Afternoon
Taxi to Tokyo EDITION · check in
Taxi from Shinjuku, 20 min. Settle in to the new room — request a Tokyo Tower view. Quick rest before sunset.
Sunset
Roppongi Hills · Mori Art Museum & Sky Deck
15-min walk from EDITION. Excellent rotating contemporary art exhibits at Mori Art Museum on the 53rd floor, then up to the open-air Sky Deck at sunset for a 360° city panorama. ¥2,000.
Evening
Cocktails at the Blue Room EDITION signature
Back at the hotel — the entire bar is bathed in deep cobalt light, one of the most photographed bars in Tokyo. Light dinner at Toranomon Hills Yokocho downstairs — a curated indoor food alley with 26 chef-driven counters: yakitori, oden, ramen, kushikatsu, gyoza. Easy and fish-free.
11 Tuesday, May 19 — Final Full Day

Yanaka · Ginza · Tokyo Tower · farewell dinner

Morning
Yanaka Ginza & Hagiso breakfast From Wanderlog
One of the few Tokyo neighborhoods that survived WWII bombings — narrow lanes, wooden houses, tiny shops, friendly cats. Walk Yanaka Ginza shopping street (good in the morning when shops are opening). Then breakfast at Hagiso — a beautifully restored 1955 wooden townhouse turned café-gallery-hotel that Grant loved, serving an elegant morning set with dashi-stock soup, seasonal rice, and ten small dishes. Allow 90 min.
Late Morning
Private tea ceremony at To-Ri-An Reservation
A genuine private ceremony in the Urasenke tradition (the most prestigious school) with English-speaking professors — Souei Funakoshi or Souai Kawada hosts. About 90 minutes, deeply meditative. Book through Wabunka or directly with the tea house. Often the most resonant cultural experience of the trip.
Lunch
Tonkatsu Maisen or Tamahide oyakodon Both fish-free
Maisen Aoyama's panko-crusted pork is iconic — kuro-buta black pork, melts under your fork. Or Tamahide in Nihonbashi for the original oyakodon (chicken & egg over rice) — they invented the dish in 1891 and have been perfecting it ever since. Both reliable and zero raw fish.
Afternoon
Ginza stroll & shopping
Walk Ginza's main artery (Chuo-dori is car-free on weekends and holidays). Stops worth making:
  • Itoya · 12-floor stationery wonderland
  • Ginza Six · contemporary luxury mall with a rooftop garden
  • Mitsukoshi Ginza · the basement food hall ("depachika") is mind-bending — sample anything from grilled wagyu skewers to perfect Japanese strawberries
  • Uniqlo Ginza Global Flagship · 12 floors
Late Afternoon
Tokyo Tower & Zojoji Temple at sunset Last view
The original Tokyo Tower (1958) — a 5-min walk from EDITION. The view is more characterful than Skytree, especially with Zojoji Temple (a major Buddhist temple from 1393) framed in front. Sunset is the move — golden hour over the city, then the tower lights up red.
Evening
Farewell dinner: yakiniku, sukiyaki or shabu-shabu Pick one
A communal, slow-paced final meal. Three excellent paths — all premium beef, all fish-free:
  • Yakiniku Jambo Hannare (Shirokanedai) — premium Japanese BBQ; you grill A5 wagyu yourself at the table. Reservation 1 month ahead via concierge. Mom & Dad will love this.
  • Ningyocho Imahan — legendary 130-year-old sukiyaki, beef & vegetables in a sweet-soy broth, dipped in raw egg. Cooked tableside in cast iron.
  • Shabuzen Roppongi — pristine wagyu shabu-shabu, you swish thinly sliced beef in a hot kombu broth and dip in sesame or ponzu sauce. Walk-in friendly with reservations.
After dinner: a quiet whisky at Bar High Five in Ginza, the city's most respected cocktail bar.

Getting Around

Logistics & Transportation

Trains between cities

  • Tokyo → Kyoto: Nozomi Shinkansen, 2hr 14min, Green Car ¥18,690 each
  • Kyoto → Odawara: Hikari Shinkansen, 2hr 10min
  • Hakone → Tokyo: Romancecar from Hakone-Yumoto to Shinjuku, 85 min

Book Shinkansen via the Smart-EX app (release 1 month ahead). Romancecar via e-romancecar.jp. JR Pass is no longer good value at current prices for this routing — buy individual tickets.

City transit

  • Suica IC card — load on iPhone Wallet before arrival
  • Tokyo Metro 72-hour pass — ¥1,500, available at most stations
  • Taxi — clean, metered, easy. Use Go (formerly JapanTaxi) app
  • Hakone Free Pass — 2 days ¥6,100 covers cable car, ropeway, pirate ship, buses

Airport transfers

  • Haneda → Mesm Tokyo: Limousine Bus, 30min, ¥1,000 pp
  • Narita → Mesm Tokyo: Narita Express to Tokyo + taxi, ~75min, ¥3,200
  • Tokyo EDITION → Haneda: Limousine Bus from Toranomon Hills, 30min

Connectivity

  • Pocket WiFi or eSIM — Ubigi or Airalo eSIM works on iPhones
  • Translation: Google Translate camera mode for menus
  • Cash: ¥30,000–¥50,000 to start. ATMs at any 7-Eleven take foreign cards

Things to Know

Travel Tips

Reservations to make in advance

  • 3+ months: Hakone Yuyado Zen (request no-fish kaiseki), Yakiniku Jambo Hannare
  • 2+ months: glänta Ring Workshop, Samoyed Cafe moffu
  • 1 month: Shinkansen Green Car seats, Sumo tickets, teamLab Planets
  • 2 weeks: Ginza Kagari, K36, kaiseki dinners, private tea ceremony
  • Same week: Shibuya Sky, Mori Art Museum

Weather & packing

  • Mid-May highs 70–75°F, lows 55–60°F. Light layers
  • Light rain possible — pack a compact umbrella (or buy ¥600 at any conbini)
  • Comfortable walking shoes are non-negotiable — Tokyo days run 15,000+ steps
  • One smart-casual outfit for kaiseki, sukiyaki & yakiniku dinners

Etiquette quick hits

  • No tipping — ever. It can confuse
  • Cash is still common at small restaurants
  • Quiet on trains. Phone calls are rude
  • Shoes off when entering ryokan rooms, traditional restaurants, temples (look for the step-up)
  • At onsen: rinse fully before entering the bath. Tattoos technically not allowed at most onsen — bring large bandage cover-ups if needed

Essentials to handle

  • Visa: US passport gets visa-free entry, 90 days
  • Visit Japan Web — pre-fill arrival forms, save 30 min at immigration
  • Marriott Bonvoy — link both hotels under the same account for points
  • Travel insurance — recommended given the trip cost
