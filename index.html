The Trip at a Glance

The Big Picture

A thoughtfully paced 10-night journey through three of Japan's most iconic destinations. You'll bookend the trip with Tokyo (modern, electric, deeply rewarding), spend three nights in Kyoto for temples, gardens, and quieter culture, then retreat to a luxury Hakone ryokan with private onsen baths. All Tokyo and Kyoto stays are Marriott Bonvoy properties — the Hakone leg is a traditional Japanese inn that's worth the detour from the points trail.

Timing Note

Mid-May is one of the loveliest stretches in Japan — Golden Week crowds have dispersed, the weather is mild (60–75°F), and you'll catch the tail of the wisteria/azalea season. Best of all, the May Grand Sumo Tournament runs May 10–24 at Ryōgoku Kokugikan in Tokyo.

HOTEL THE MITSUI KYOTO ↗
May 13 – 16 (3 nights)

Awarded Three Michelin Keys in 2025 and named to The World's 50 Best Hotels — and rightly so. Built on a 250-year-old Mitsui family site directly across from Nijō Castle, the hotel has its own natural hot spring drawn from 1,000 meters below ground. The communal "Thermal Spring" pools (swimsuits worn) are unlike anything you'll find at another hotel — a perfect end-of-day soak after temple touring. Rooms are serene, the breakfast is excellent, and Chef Asano's Kyoto-French restaurant TOKI is a destination in itself.

One of the most stylish modern ryokans in Hakone, just a two-minute walk from Gora Station. Ultra-contemporary architecture meets traditional ryokan service — every room has its own private open-air onsen on a balcony, and there are two beautifully designed public open-air baths plus three additional private onsens by reservation. Walkable to Hakone Open-Air Museum and Gora Park; quick cable car / ropeway to Owakudani and Lake Ashi.

Activities · pick what fits

Sensō-ji Temple & Nakamise Street
Asakusa · 90 min · free · 25-min taxi from hotel

Tokyo's oldest Buddhist temple (645 AD). Enter through the giant red Kaminarimon lantern gate, walk Nakamise Dori with 89 stalls. Afternoon is actually nicer — cooler light, fewer tour groups.

Hamarikyu Gardens
Tokyo Bay · 60 min · ¥300 · 10-min taxi from hotel

17th-century shogunal garden surrounded by skyscrapers, with a teahouse on a pond. Quiet, contemplative, easy on tired legs.

Food & drinks · pick what fits

Asakusa Imahan
Sukiyaki · No fish · walks to Sensō-ji

130-year-old sukiyaki house. Premium beef cooked tableside in cast iron, dipped in raw egg. Eat early (5–6 pm) to fight jet lag — they can crash by 9.

Sometaro
Okonomiyaki · Casual · Asakusa

Japanese cabbage-and-pork pancakes you cook yourself on a tabletop griddle. Fun, casual alternative to Imahan.

Toranomon Hills Yokocho
Food alley · At the EDITION · easy default

26 chef-driven counters: yakitori, oden, ramen, kushikatsu, gyoza. Crash-friendly if Asakusa felt too far.

Blue Room nightcap
Cocktails · In the lobby

The cobalt-lit cocktail bar — one of Tokyo's most photographed. Skip if energy's gone. Otherwise a perfect 30-min wind-down.

Katsudon-ya Zuicho
Pork cutlet · Shibuya · 5-min walk from Sky · queue 30–45 min

Tsubaki Salon

Breaded beef cutlet, sliced rare, finish each piece on a hot stone at the table. No reservations, 15–30 min wait. Skip if exhausted after teamLab.

Activities · sumo anchors the day

Sumo Tournament — guided tour at 13:00
Booked · Ryōgoku Kokugikan · 13:00–18:00 · B seating

Meet Klook guide at the Kokugikan entrance. Lower divisions early, entrance ceremony ~3pm, top-division (makuuchi) bouts 3:30–6 pm. Box seats = tatami boxes near the floor (shoes off, shared with another pair). Snacks/drinks allowed.

Imperial Palace East Gardens
Marunouchi · 90 min · free · before sumo

Former Edo Castle grounds, flat paths through stone walls and Japanese gardens. Azalea blooms in mid-May. Wrap by 11:30 to leave time for Tsukiji lunch + Ryōgoku transit.

Sumō Museum
Free · next to Kokugikan

Inside the Kokugikan complex — exhibits on legendary wrestlers, kesho-mawashi ceremonial aprons, tournament history. Quick 30-min stop before/after the bouts.

Food & drinks · pick what fits

Tsukiji Outer Market food crawl
Lunch · 5-min taxi from Imperial Palace · 20-min taxi to Ryōgoku

Tamago on a stick, wagyu skewers, A5 beef croquettes, dashimaki tamago, melon-pan, fresh strawberries. Graze 6–8 stalls. Eat by 12:15 to make the sumo meet-up.

Chanko Tomoegata
Sumo hot pot · walks from Kokugikan · dinner

The traditional one-pot stew sumo wrestlers eat to bulk up. Run by a former wrestler. Healthy, delicious, and the perfect dinner after the bouts.

Bar High Five nightcap
Cocktails · Ginza · 15-min taxi from EDITION

Tokyo's most awarded cocktail bar. A quiet whisky to close out a sumo-and-Tsukiji day. Walk-in friendly with reservation.

Activities · the boat anchors the day

Hozugawa River Boat Ride
Booked · 15:00–17:00 · departs Kameoka, ends Arashiyama

Traditional wooden boat, three boatmen poling through the river gorge — small rapids, calm pools, forested mountains. Bring a light layer (river breeze cool even in May) and water. Snacks allowed.

Mitsui Thermal Spring spa
At the hotel · post-boat soak · ~30–60 min

Natural onsen drawn from 1,000m below ground, swimsuit-required communal pool. The perfect post-boat unwind before dinner.

Nishiki Market evening crawl
15-min walk from Mitsui · open till 6 pm

If you arrive back early enough — graze 5 blocks of stalls (yuba tofu, soy donuts, dashimaki tamago, wagyu skewers). Skip if you'd rather rest.

Food & drinks · pick what fits

Ekiben on the Shinkansen
Tokyo Station Daimaru basement · grab before boarding

Bento boxes from the Daimaru basement are a Shinkansen tradition. Pick one up before boarding for an in-seat lunch with Mt. Fuji views.

Yukichi Honpo
Kyoto ramen · 10-min walk from Mitsui · light lunch

Refined Kyoto-style chicken-broth ramen — clear, bright, lighter than Tokyo tonkotsu. Quick lunch between arrival and the boat.

Mishima-tei Pontocho
Sukiyaki · Pontocho · dinner · reservation needed

Legendary 150-year-old Kyoto sukiyaki — premium beef cooked tableside in cast iron, dipped in raw egg. May is yuka (riverside terrace) season — request one.

Kyoto Tonkatsu Katsuda
Tonkatsu · Pontocho · walk-in friendly · alt to Mishima-tei

Crispy Kyoto-style breaded pork cutlet. Easier reservation than Mishima-tei, casual but excellent.

Activities · teamLab anchors, Kyoto unfolds

teamLab Biovortex Kyoto
Booked · flex pass anytime · 7-min walk Kyoto Station Hachijo East · 2 hrs inside

The new flagship teamLab (opened Oct 2025, 10,000 sqm, 50+ environmental-art installations). Largest teamLab in Japan. Best in the morning when fresh.

Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion)
NW Kyoto · 45 min · ¥500 · 25-min taxi from central

The actual gold-leafed temple reflecting on its mirror pond. Quick visit, big visual payoff. Pair with teamLab in one half-day for the two photogenic Kyoto landmarks.

Higashiyama walk
SE Kyoto · 2–3 hrs · all walkable in sequence

Kiyomizu-dera (UNESCO, ¥400) → walk down Sannenzaka + Ninenzaka (preserved stone streets, old wooden shops) → past Yasaka Pagoda → into Gion. The single most cinematic Kyoto walk — all sequential, no transit between.

K36 Rooftop bar at sunset
Higashiyama · directly across from Yasaka Pagoda

Best sunset cocktail spot in Kyoto. Old roofs and the pagoda lit up below. Slot in after the Higashiyama walk before dinner.

Gion stroll at dusk
Continues from Higashiyama walk · free

Around 6 pm you may spot real geiko/maiko commuting to evening engagements. Most photogenic Kyoto streets at lantern-light hour.

Food & drinks · pick what fits

Sannenzaka dessert crawl

Gion Karyo
Kaiseki · in Gion machiya · dinner · reservation

Toki at Mitsui

Chef Asano's "Kyoto French" — easiest top-tier kaiseki since you're staying upstairs. Good if you'd rather dinner near the hotel.

Arashiyama Bamboo Grove + Tenryū-ji
Tour stop · NW Kyoto · ~90 min

Famous bamboo grove walk and likely a stop at Tenryū-ji or Togetsukyo Bridge. The bamboo and the Zen pond are right next to each other.

Fushimi Inari Taisha
Tour stop · S Kyoto · ~60–90 min

The famous tunnel of 10,000 vermillion torii gates climbing the mountain. Tour will likely take you partway up — you can climb further if energy.

Tōdai-ji & Nara Park

A natural full circle: Open-Air Museum (Gora, walk-to) → cable car up to Sounzan → Ropeway over Owakudani → continue down to Togendai on Lake Ashi → lunch lakeside → Pirate Ship cruise across Lake Ashi → Hakone Shrine torii → bus back to Gora → walk through Gora Park to the ryokan. Bus, cable car, ropeway, cruise, all on the Free Pass.

Activities · pick whatever fits, in any order

Sunrise private onsen
Don't skip · in-room balcony · pre-breakfast

Wake early. Long soak in your room's private balcony onsen as morning fog rises off the Hakone forest. Best onsen of the trip.

Hakone Open-Air Museum
Highlight · 5-min walk from ryokan · 2 hrs · ¥1,800

70,000 sqm of outdoor sculpture park — Henry Moore, Rodin, Miro, plus a dedicated Picasso Pavilion with 300+ works. Free hot-spring foot bath in the middle.

Hakone Ropeway over Owakudani
Volcanic valley · Free Pass covers it · gondola views

Cable car from Gora up to Sounzan, then ropeway over the active volcanic valley. Sulfur steam vents, black rock, and on a clear day huge Mt. Fuji views from the gondola.

Pirate Ship Lake Ashi cruise
35 min · Togendai → Motohakone-ko · Free Pass

Continue the ropeway down to Togendai. Camp pirate-ship aesthetic, totally fun — 35-min cruise across Lake Ashi.

Hakone Shrine torii
Iconic photo · 10-min walk from Motohakone-ko

Famous floating red torii gate in Lake Ashi with Fuji potentially behind it. Photo line can be 30+ min — mid-afternoon sometimes calmer than mornings.

Hakone Gora Park walk
5-min walk back to the ryokan · ¥550 (Free Pass)

Japan's first Western-style park — fountain, rose terraces, traditional teahouse, glass greenhouse. Walk through it on the way back from the bus stop. The ryokan is just at the top of the path.

Spa massage at Gora KARAKU
Booked at check-in · 60–90 min · in-room or spa

If you booked yesterday — slot in the morning before the loop, or the late afternoon before kaiseki. The hour before dinner is the prime onsen + spa window.

Pola Museum of Art
Optional · alternative to Open-Air · Sengoku

Excellent Impressionist collection in a forested glass building near Sengoku. A quieter half-day alternative if Open-Air feels too much.

Food & drinks

La Terrazza Ashinoko
Lakeside lunch · Togendai · pasta with a Lake Ashi view

Things to Know

Travel Tips

Reservations to make in advance

  • 3+ months: Hakone Gora KARAKU (request no-fish kaiseki), Yakiniku Jambo Hannare
  • 2+ months: glänta Ring Workshop, Samoyed Cafe moffu
  • 1 month: Shinkansen Green Car seats, Sumo tickets, teamLab Planets
  • 2 weeks: Ginza Kagari, K36, Shodai (Ebisu), kaiseki dinners, private tea ceremony
  • Same week: Shibuya Sky, Mori Art Museum

Weather & packing

  • Mid-May highs 70–75°F, lows 55–60°F. Light layers
  • Light rain possible — pack a compact umbrella (or buy ¥600 at any conbini)
  • Comfortable walking shoes are non-negotiable — Tokyo days run 15,000+ steps
  • One smart-casual outfit for kaiseki, sukiyaki & yakiniku dinners

Etiquette quick hits

  • No tipping — ever. It can confuse
  • Cash is still common at small restaurants
  • Quiet on trains. Phone calls are rude
  • Shoes off when entering ryokan rooms, traditional restaurants, temples (look for the step-up)
  • At onsen: rinse fully before entering the bath. Tattoos technically not allowed at most onsen — bring large bandage cover-ups if needed

Essentials to handle

  • Visa: US passport gets visa-free entry, 90 days
  • Visit Japan Web — pre-fill arrival forms, save 30 min at immigration
  • Marriott Bonvoy — link both hotels under the same account for points
  • Travel insurance — recommended given the trip cost

Mt. Fuji viewing window

  • Best chance: early morning (sunrise–9 am) when air is clear
  • Worst chance: late afternoon — clouds typically build up around the summit
  • May visibility: roughly 50% of mornings; cloudy days happen
  • Strategy: wake early at the ryokan and check the bath view at sunrise. If Fuji is out, drop everything and look

Shinkansen luggage rules

  • Bags ≤160 cm (L+W+H) — fit overhead, no reservation needed
  • Bags 160–250 cm — must reserve a "tokudai-tedaisu" oversized luggage seat at booking (free, but limited)
  • Bags >250 cm — not allowed (most large suitcases are fine)
  • Tip: pack one large checked bag + one carry-on per person to stay safely under 160 cm

Apps to install before you go

  • Google Maps — best for transit routing in Japan
  • GO (formerly JapanTaxi) — best taxi-hailing app, English-friendly
  • Smart-EX — Shinkansen booking, QR boarding
  • Suica on Apple Wallet — load before arriving, tap-to-ride on all trains/buses
  • Visit Japan Web — pre-fill arrival forms, save 30 min at immigration
  • Google Translate — camera mode reads menus instantly
  • Marriott Bonvoy — for digital keys + concierge messaging

Money & payments

  • Bring ¥30,000–¥50,000 per person to start, get more from ATMs as needed
  • 7-Eleven and Family Mart ATMs accept all foreign cards 24/7
  • Most restaurants/hotels take credit cards, but small ramen shops, temples, and taxis often need cash
  • No tipping — never. Service charge is built in everywhere
  • Tax-free shopping at major stores: bring passport, spend ¥5,000+
