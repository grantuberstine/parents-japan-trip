The Trip at a Glance

The Big Picture

A thoughtfully paced 10-night journey through three of Japan's most iconic destinations. You'll bookend the trip with Tokyo (modern, electric, deeply rewarding), spend three nights in Kyoto for temples, gardens, and quieter culture, then retreat to a luxury Hakone ryokan with private onsen baths. All Tokyo and Kyoto stays are Marriott Bonvoy properties — the Hakone leg is a traditional Japanese inn that's worth the detour from the points trail.

Timing Note

Mid-May is one of the loveliest stretches in Japan — Golden Week crowds have dispersed, the weather is mild (60–75°F), and you'll catch the tail of the wisteria/azalea season. Best of all, the May Grand Sumo Tournament runs May 10–24 at Ryōgoku Kokugikan in Tokyo — built right into Day 3 of the itinerary.

Awarded Three Michelin Keys in 2025 and named to The World's 50 Best Hotels — and rightly so. Built on a 250-year-old Mitsui family site directly across from Nijō Castle, the hotel has its own natural hot spring drawn from 1,000 meters below ground. Day by Day

The Itinerary

Each day is paced with one or two anchor activities, a leisurely lunch window, and an evening that's never rushed. Most days end before 7 pm so there's room for a relaxed dinner and an early-ish night. Mornings are deliberately light to start.

Morning
Land at Haneda & transfer to EDITION
Most US flights from the West Coast land at Haneda (HND) around 9–11 am after the overnight crossing. Customs + bag claim takes about 30 min. Easiest transfer: Airport Limousine Bus (¥1,200, ~40 min direct to Toranomon Hills) or taxi (¥7,000–9,000 with luggage). Drop bags at the EDITION concierge if the room isn't ready (3 pm check-in).
Afternoon
Sensō-ji Temple & Nakamise Street Iconic
Tokyo's oldest Buddhist temple (founded 645 AD). 25-min taxi from the EDITION. Enter through the giant red Kaminarimon lantern gate, walk Nakamise Dori with its 89 stalls of crackers, fans, and sweets, and arrive at the main hall. Afternoon arrival is fine — cooler light, fewer tour groups than morning. Allow about 90 minutes.
Early Dinner
Asakusa Imahan — sukiyaki No fish
A 130-year-old institution serving classic sukiyaki — premium beef cooked tableside in cast iron with sweet-soy broth, dipped in raw egg. Lighter lunch sets available. Walking distance from Sensō-ji, no reservation needed but go right at opening to beat the wave. Eat early (5–6 pm) so they can crash by 9 — fights the jet lag.

Alt: Sometaro for okonomiyaki — Japanese cabbage-and-pork pancakes you cook yourself on a tabletop griddle. Casual and fun.
Evening
Back to the EDITION · early to bed
Taxi back to Toranomon. Optional nightcap at the Blue Room in the lobby — the cobalt-lit cocktail bar — but skip if energy is gone. Sleep early. Tomorrow is a big day.

Morning
Imperial Palace East Gardens
The grounds of the former Edo Castle, free entry, mostly flat paths through stone walls and Japanese gardens. About 90 minutes is plenty. Beautiful azalea blooms in mid-May. Aim to wrap by 11:30 am so there's time for lunch and getting to the sumo meet-up.
Lunch
Tsukiji Outer Market food crawl Highlight
Inner market moved to Toyosu, but the outer market is still a wonderland — tamago (sweet egg) on a stick, wagyu skewers, A5 beef croquettes, dashimaki tamago, melon-pan, fresh strawberries, oysters if they want them. Graze through 6–8 stalls. Eat by 12:15, then taxi to Ryōgoku (20 min) for the sumo meet-up.
13:00 — 18:00
Grand Sumo Tournament — guided tour Booked
Meet the Klook guide at 13:00 at the Ryōgoku Kokugikan entrance. The guide explains the rules and ritual, then you watch the lower divisions, the entrance ceremony around 3 pm, and the top-division (makuuchi) bouts from 3:30–6 pm in your reserved Box B seats. Bring the e-voucher (link).

Box seats are 4-person tatami boxes near the floor — shoes off, shared with another pair. Snacks and drinks allowed inside the arena. Final ceremony around 6 pm.
Dinner
Chanko nabe in Ryōgoku
The traditional one-pot stew sumo wrestlers eat to bulk up — surprisingly delicious and healthy. Chanko Tomoegata (run by a former wrestler) is the classic move. Walking distance from the arena. After dinner, taxi back to the EDITION (~25 min).

The lantern-lit alley along the Kamo River — Kyoto at its most cinematic. Mishima-tei Pontocho is the legendary 150-year-old sukiyaki house — premium beef cooked tableside in cast iron, dipped in raw egg. Reservation needed. May is yuka (riverside terrace) season — request one.

Transfer at Hakone-Yumoto to the Hakone Tozan railway — a scenic 40-min switchback ride climbing through forested mountains to Gora Station. Or taxi (~30 min). Gora KARAKU is a 2-min walk from the station.
Afternoon
Check in Hakone Gora KARAKU Onsen + spa moment
Slip into the yukata robes provided in the room. The ryokan has three layers of onsen — pick whichever feels right:
  • Your room's private balcony onsen — open-air, looking into the Hakone forest. Use it whenever you want, day or night.
  • The two public open-air baths (separate men's/women's) — beautifully designed with mountain views, included with the stay.
  • Three private onsens by reservation — ¥5,000/hour, can be booked at check-in for a slot before dinner. Couples can use these together.
Book the spa massage at check-in for the next morning or late afternoon — Gora KARAKU offers in-room and spa-room treatments (typical 60–90 min Japanese-style relaxation massage). Reservations fill up fast, so ask the front desk first thing.
Evening
Kaiseki dinner in-room 10+ courses
Multi-course traditional dinner served by the room attendant — appetizers, simmered dishes, Hakone mountain vegetables, hot pot, grilled wagyu, rice course, dessert. A wonderful, slow ritual.

Important: contact the ryokan at booking to request a no-fish kaiseki menu — they will substitute beef/chicken/vegetable courses for the fish/seafood components. Most ryokans accommodate this with 7+ days notice. After dinner, one more onsen soak under the stars.

Pre-breakfast
Sunrise private onsen Don't skip
Wake early. Long soak in your room's private balcony onsen as the morning fog rises off the Hakone forest. Western breakfast at the ryokan around 8.
Morning
Hakone Open-Air Museum Highlight · 5-min walk
Walk over from the ryokan — the museum is right next to Gora Station. 70,000 sqm of outdoor sculpture park with works by Henry Moore, Rodin, Miro — plus a dedicated Picasso Pavilion with 300+ works. There's also a free hot-spring foot bath in the middle of the park. About 2 hours. ¥1,800.
Late Morning
Hakone cable car up to Ropeway & Owakudani Volcanic valley
From Gora Station take the historic cable car up to Sounzan, then transfer to the Hakone Ropeway over the active volcanic valley. Sulfur steam vents, black rock, and (on a clear day) huge Mt. Fuji views from the gondola. Get off at Owakudani Station and try the kuro-tamago (eggs hard-boiled in the sulfur springs — said to add 7 years to your life).
Lunch / Cruise
Hakone Sightseeing Cruise (Pirate Ship) on Lake Ashi
Continue the ropeway down to Togendai on Lake Ashi. Quick lunch at the lakeside (La Terrazza Ashinoko for pasta with a view, or grab onigiri). Then board the pirate ship — kind of camp, totally fun — 35-min cruise across the lake to Motohakone-ko. ¥1,200. All of this (cable car + ropeway + cruise + buses back) is included in the Hakone Free Pass (¥6,100, worth it).
Mid Afternoon
Hakone Shrine torii on Lake Ashi Iconic photo
From Motohakone-ko walk 10 min to Hakone Shrine — the famous floating red torii gate in Lake Ashi with Fuji potentially behind it. Photo line in season can be 30+ min; mid-afternoon is sometimes calmer than mornings here.
Late Afternoon
Walk through Hakone Gora Park back to the ryokan Don't skip
Hakone Free Pass bus back to Gora Station (~30 min). From there, walk through Hakone Gora Park on your way to the ryokan — Japan's first Western-style park, with a fountain, rose terraces, traditional teahouse, and a glass greenhouse with tropical plants. The path winds gently uphill past the gardens, and the ryokan is just at the top. ¥550 entry (or covered by Hakone Free Pass — show your pass). About 45 minutes through the park, then 5-min walk to Gora KARAKU.
Pre-Dinner
Spa massage or private onsen reservation Booked at check-in
Whatever you booked yesterday — slot it in here before kaiseki. Either:
  • 60–90 min spa massage in your room or the spa
  • Private onsen room for an hour (¥5,000) — different feel from your room's balcony bath
  • Or just an extended soak in the public open-air baths and the in-room onsen
The hour before dinner is the prime onsen window — you want the heat to settle before the meal.
Evening
Back to ryokan · second kaiseki
Different menu tonight — the chef changes the kaiseki nightly so the second dinner is a fresh experience. One more long onsen and an early bedtime.

Morning
Yanaka Ginza & Hagiso breakfast From Wanderlog
One of the few Tokyo neighborhoods that survived WWII bombings — narrow lanes, wooden houses, tiny shops, friendly cats. Walk Yanaka Ginza shopping street (good in the morning when shops are opening). Then breakfast at Hagiso — a beautifully restored 1955 wooden townhouse turned café-gallery-hotel that Grant loved, serving an elegant morning set with dashi-stock soup, seasonal rice, and ten small dishes. Late Morning
Private tea ceremony at To-Ri-An Reservation
A genuine private ceremony in the Urasenke tradition (the most prestigious school) with English-speaking professors — Souei Funakoshi or Souai Kawada hosts. About 90 minutes, deeply meditative. Book through Wabunka or directly with the tea house. Often the most resonant cultural experience of the trip.
Lunch
Tonkatsu Maisen or Tamahide oyakodon Both fish-free
Maisen Aoyama's panko-crusted pork is iconic — kuro-buta black pork, melts under your fork. Or Tamahide in Nihonbashi for the original oyakodon (chicken & egg over rice) — they invented the dish in 1891 and have been perfecting it ever since. Both reliable and zero raw fish.
Afternoon
Ginza stroll & shopping
Walk Ginza's main artery (Chuo-dori is car-free on weekends and holidays). Stops worth making:
  • Itoya · 12-floor stationery wonderland
  • Ginza Six · contemporary luxury mall with a rooftop garden
  • Mitsukoshi Ginza · the basement food hall ("depachika") is mind-bending — sample anything from grilled wagyu skewers to perfect Japanese strawberries
  • Uniqlo Ginza Global Flagship · 12 floors
Late Afternoon
Tokyo Tower & Zojoji Temple at sunset Last view
The original Tokyo Tower (1958) — 15 min by taxi from the JW Marriott. The view is more characterful than Skytree, especially with Zojoji Temple (a major Buddhist temple from 1393) framed in front. Sunset is the move — golden hour over the city, then the tower lights up red.
Evening
Farewell dinner — Shodai (Ebisu) Wanderlog must-have
Top pick: Shodai 焼塩 in Ebisu — modern kappo-style restaurant Grant rates as a top Tokyo experience. Their signature beef udon with potato foam is the dish — wagyu + handmade udon under a cloud of light potato foam. Modern, refined, distinctly Japanese. Counter seating, ~14 seats. Reservation required well ahead.

If Shodai is full, three reliable backups:
  • Yakiniku Jambo Hannare (Shirokanedai) — premium Japanese BBQ; you grill A5 wagyu yourself at the table. Concierge reserve 1 month ahead.
  • Ningyocho Imahan — legendary 130-year-old sukiyaki, beef & vegetables in a sweet-soy broth, dipped in raw egg.
  • Shabuzen Roppongi — pristine wagyu shabu-shabu, you swish thinly sliced beef in a hot kombu broth.
After dinner: a quiet whisky at Bar High Five in Ginza, the city's most respected cocktail bar.

Things to Know

Travel Tips

Reservations to make in advance

  • 3+ months: Hakone Gora KARAKU (request no-fish kaiseki), Yakiniku Jambo Hannare
  • 2+ months: glänta Ring Workshop, Samoyed Cafe moffu
  • 1 month: Shinkansen Green Car seats, Sumo tickets, teamLab Planets
  • 2 weeks: Ginza Kagari, K36, Shodai (Ebisu), kaiseki dinners, private tea ceremony
  • Same week: Shibuya Sky, Mori Art Museum

Weather & packing

  • Mid-May highs 70–75°F, lows 55–60°F. Light layers
  • Light rain possible — pack a compact umbrella (or buy ¥600 at any conbini)
  • Comfortable walking shoes are non-negotiable — Tokyo days run 15,000+ steps
  • One smart-casual outfit for kaiseki, sukiyaki & yakiniku dinners

Etiquette quick hits

  • No tipping — ever. It can confuse
  • Cash is still common at small restaurants
  • Quiet on trains. Phone calls are rude
  • Shoes off when entering ryokan rooms, traditional restaurants, temples (look for the step-up)
  • At onsen: rinse fully before entering the bath. Tattoos technically not allowed at most onsen — bring large bandage cover-ups if needed

Essentials to handle

  • Visa: US passport gets visa-free entry, 90 days
  • Visit Japan Web — pre-fill arrival forms, save 30 min at immigration
  • Marriott Bonvoy — link both hotels under the same account for points
  • Travel insurance — recommended given the trip cost

Mt. Fuji viewing window

  • Best chance: early morning (sunrise–9 am) when air is clear
  • Worst chance: late afternoon — clouds typically build up around the summit
  • May visibility: roughly 50% of mornings; cloudy days happen
  • Strategy: wake early at the ryokan and check the bath view at sunrise. If Fuji is out, drop everything and look

Shinkansen luggage rules

  • Bags ≤160 cm (L+W+H) — fit overhead, no reservation needed
  • Bags 160–250 cm — must reserve a "tokudai-tedaisu" oversized luggage seat at booking (free, but limited)
  • Bags >250 cm — not allowed (most large suitcases are fine)
  • Tip: pack one large checked bag + one carry-on per person to stay safely under 160 cm

Apps to install before you go

  • Google Maps — best for transit routing in Japan
  • GO (formerly JapanTaxi) — best taxi-hailing app, English-friendly
  • Smart-EX — Shinkansen booking, QR boarding
  • Suica on Apple Wallet — load before arriving, tap-to-ride on all trains/buses
  • Visit Japan Web — pre-fill arrival forms, save 30 min at immigration
  • Google Translate — camera mode reads menus instantly
  • Marriott Bonvoy — for digital keys + concierge messaging

Money & payments

  • Bring ¥30,000–¥50,000 per person to start, get more from ATMs as needed
  • 7-Eleven and Family Mart ATMs accept all foreign cards 24/7
  • Most restaurants/hotels take credit cards, but small ramen shops, temples, and taxis often need cash
  • No tipping — never. Service charge is built in everywhere
  • Tax-free shopping at major stores: bring passport, spend ¥5,000+
